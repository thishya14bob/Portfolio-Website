01. Where it started

It started with my grandfather.

He spent an entire day trying to submit his life certificate. A life certificate is how a pensioner confirms they're alive, so their payments don't stop.

He wasn't struggling with the task. He was struggling with a system that said nothing back.

"I kept trying to scan my finger… it just didn't tell me what I was doing wrong."

02. Research

I thought it was a product problem.

My first guess was the fingerprint scanner: improve the hardware, improve the sensitivity. But the experience involves two people, the pensioner and the operator who assists them. So I interviewed both.

Sita Aunty, a pensioner since 2017, and Rajesh Ji, a Meeseva operator since 2021

The problem wasn't the product. It was the interaction between user, system, and guidance.

03. Mapping the pain

What happens when the system won't talk back?

I mapped what pensioners say, think, do and feel during verification. When a scan failed, they didn't just retry. They turned to operators for help, which made them more dependent.

The real problem: no clear feedback, and heavy dependency on operators.

Where users struggle: biometric verification and assisted data entry.

Why it matters: every failure adds anxiety and pushes people towards help.

04. The journey

05. The solution

A guided, self-service kiosk.

How might we enable pensioners to complete biometric verification independently, with confidence?

The Meeseva Assisted Kiosk sits at CSC centres and targets the two biggest pain points.

1. Biometric first, then auto-fetch

Instead of manual data entry by an operator, the pensioner scans first. Their details are fetched automatically and they simply confirm them.

2. Real-time feedback while scanning

Instead of a silent failure, the kiosk explains what went wrong and how to fix it: wipe a moist finger, press more firmly, or reposition. If it still fails, it offers an alternate verification or help.

06. Feedback

07. What's next

This is just the starting point.

The real barrier wasn't technology. It was the lack of guidance around it.

Failure is rarely the problem. Unclear feedback is.

Independence comes from guidance, not just simplification.

In Studio 2, I'll take these insights into designing and testing solutions for truly independent verification.
