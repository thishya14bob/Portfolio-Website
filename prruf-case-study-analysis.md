## Part 2: Analysis

### What's working well
1. **Strong, human hook.** "What if your laptop just remembered for you?" plus a personal origin story (you abandoned your own log) is relatable and honest.
2. **A clear point of view.** The "not surveillance" positioning is the most distinctive thing on the page. The comparison table and privacy rules make an abstract value concrete.
3. **Show, don't tell.** The real walkthrough video, three feature clips and a clickable walkthrough built in code put far more product on the page than most student case studies.
4. **Social proof.** Four named testers from real studios, in a format that fits the product's personality.
5. **Consistent visual language.** The Prruf green, yellow chat bubbles, Mac windows and terminal styling all feel like one brand.

### Gaps to fix (priority order)

**1. Three empty image placeholders: highest priority.**
These look unfinished to a recruiter:
- "The abandoned two-week manual log → the Prruf dashboard" (section 01).
- "Three screens: Sessions / Session / Report" (section 02).
- "Observer, not warden" illustration (section 05).

Either add the visuals or remove the placeholders.

**2. Numbers don't match each other.**
- "~2.5 min between captures" in the stats vs "Captures every 3 min" in the walkthrough.
- "5+ studios" in the stats vs "Five studios and a handful of freelancers" in testers. These are fine together, but say it the same way.
- "~10 tasks avg per session": is this measured? If not, label it an estimate or remove it.

**3. It reads like a product page more than a design case study.**
Recruiters want to see *your design thinking*. Currently missing:
- **Process:** sketches, wireframes, early versions, what changed and why.
- **Research:** how many people you spoke to and what you learned. Section 05 hints that feedback shaped the privacy safeguards; show that moment.
- **Design decisions:** e.g. why text-only storage, why a break detector, why chat-bubble onboarding. One or two "We tried X → it failed → so we did Y" stories would massively strengthen it.
- **Your role vs Tanish's:** "Founder, Product Designer, Builder" is broad. Say what *you* specifically designed or built.

**4. No outcome or reflection.**
- There's no "what we learned / what's next" ending.
- No measurable result, e.g. "testers logged X hours without writing a single note" or "Y of 5 studios asked to keep using it".

**5. Small content details.**
- The "Studio Project" tag is confusing for a real product with a co-founder. Consider "Startup" or "Live product".
- The walkthrough shows three "Untitled session · 0 tasks" items. Realistic names, like "Client deck – Week 3", would look more alive.
- "Jamalo" appears in the walkthrough placeholder text. Check whether this is a real client name or a typo.
- The model name "claude-sonnet-5" in the mock UI is a technical detail visitors don't need. Consider "AI model" or removing it.
- The feature clips have mute icons, but they're animated images with no sound now, so the icons can be removed.

**6. Length.**
Seven sections plus video, four feature rows, an interactive walkthrough, a terminal and four testimonials make a long page. On mobile it's about 8,800px tall. Consider:
- Combining 02 (What it does) into 03 (Features).
- Trimming the stats to the two strongest.

### Suggested new structure
1. Hero + one-line problem.
2. **The problem**: your story and Tanish's (existing 01).
3. **Research & insight**: who you talked to and the key fear people had (new).
4. **Design decisions**: 2–3 decisions with before → after (new).
5. **The product**: video + feature clips + walkthrough (existing 03 + 04).
6. **Privacy by design**: existing 05.
7. **Testing & feedback**: existing 06, plus one measurable result.
8. **What I learned / what's next** (new).
